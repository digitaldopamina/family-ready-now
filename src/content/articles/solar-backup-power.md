  - question: "How long do solar panels last?"
    answer: "Modern solar panels are warrantied for 25–30 years, with an annual efficiency degradation of 0.5–0.7%. A 400W panel installed today will still produce about 320–340W after 25 years. Inverters and batteries have shorter lifespans: inverters 10–15 years, batteries 5–15 years depending on chemistry and cycling. These are the components you'll replace during a system's lifetime, not the panels themselves."
  - question: "Does solar work on cloudy days?"
    answer: "Yes, with reduced output. On overcast days, panels typically produce 10–25% of rated capacity. Partly cloudy skies yield 40–60%. On a string of cloudy winter days in a northern climate, you can expect enough output to maintain essential loads, but not to power high-demand appliances. Factor your local climate into sizing — Seattle requires a larger system than Phoenix for the same output."

A power outage exposes how dependent our lives are on grid electricity. Solar power with battery storage lets you keep critical systems running — refrigerator, medical equipment, lighting, phone charging, internet — through outages that last hours or days.

This guide covers the full range, from a $1,000 portable power station that handles the basics to a $15,000+ whole-home backup system.

## Understanding the Options: Portable vs. Whole-Home

**Portable solar power stations** are self-contained units: battery storage + inverter + charging circuits in one box, with optional solar panels. They're plug-and-play, require no installation, and can be used anywhere. Capacity ranges from 300Wh (charge your phone several times) to 5,000+Wh (run a refrigerator for a few days). Cost: $300–$5,000.

**Whole-home solar + battery systems** are permanently installed, integrate with your electrical panel, and can power your home automatically when the grid goes down. Some systems (like Tesla Powerwall or Enphase IQ) provide seamless backup — your refrigerator never even turns off during an outage. Cost: $10,000–$30,000+ installed.

**What most families actually need:** A mid-range portable power station (1,000–2,000Wh) with a 100–200W solar panel for recharging covers emergency use cases at $500–1,500 — a fraction of whole-home cost with no installation required.

## Step 1: Calculate Your Emergency Power Needs

Before buying anything, figure out what you actually need to run during an outage.

| Device | Wattage | Hours/Day | Daily kWh |
|--------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Refrigerator | 100–400W (avg ~150) | 24 (runs ~8h) | 1–2 kWh |
| Freezer | 100–300W | 24 (runs ~8h) | 0.5–1.5 kWh |
| LED lighting (5 bulbs) | 50W total | 6 | 0.3 kWh |
| Phone charging | 20W | 2 | 0.04 kWh |
| Laptop | 45–65W | 4 | 0.2–0.26 kWh |
| CPAP machine | 30–60W | 8 | 0.24–0.5 kWh |
| Box fan | 25–75W | 8 | 0.2–0.6 kWh |

**Essential emergency loads for most families:** Refrigerator + lighting + phone charging + laptop + CPAP if applicable = approximately 2–4 kWh per day.

A 2,000Wh (2kWh) battery holds about 1 day of these essentials. With solar recharging, it's indefinitely sustainable on sunny days and lasts 2–3 days through a cloudy period.

## Portable Power Stations: The Flexible Option

These units have transformed emergency power for most households. No installation, no permits, no complexity.

- Jackery Explorer 2000 Pro (2,160Wh, 2,200W)
- EcoFlow Delta 2 Max (2,048Wh, 2,400W)

- EcoFlow Delta Pro (3,600Wh, 3,600W, expandable)

### Matching Solar Panels to Your Power Station

A 200W solar panel generates approximately 1 kWh on a good sunny day (5 hours of effective sun). To fully charge a 2,000Wh battery bank in a day, you'd need 400W of panels under ideal conditions — or accept partial recharging.

**Practical rule:** Buy 100–200W of solar capacity per 1,000Wh of battery storage if you want reasonable recharging. More is always better.

**Foldable panels:** EcoFlow, Jackery, and Bluetti all make panels designed to connect directly to their power stations. They're convenient but pricey per watt. DIY alternatives with separate panels + adapters can cut costs significantly.

## Permanent Home Systems

For whole-home backup, the landscape has improved significantly in the past 5 years.

### Battery-Only Backup (No Solar)

**Tesla Powerwall 3:** 13.5 kWh capacity, 11.5 kW continuous output, can back up a whole home. ~$9,000 for the unit, plus $2,000–5,000 installation. Without solar, it charges from the grid at night (off-peak rates) and discharges during outages. A single Powerwall handles essential loads for 1–2 days; two handle more.

**Enphase IQ Battery:** 10.5 kWh (stackable). Pairs with Enphase microinverters. ~$8,000–12,000 installed.

**LG RESU Prime:** 9.6–16 kWh. Compatible with multiple inverter brands.

### Solar + Battery: The Complete System

A grid-tied solar system with battery backup is the gold standard for energy resilience:
- Powers your home normally from solar during the day
- Exports excess to the grid for credits (net metering)
- Stores excess in batteries for evening and outages
- Grid serves as unlimited backup on cloudy weeks

**What it costs:**
- 7 kW solar system: ~$14,000–20,000 before incentives
- Powerwall or equivalent: $8,000–13,000
- Total installed: $22,000–33,000 before incentives

**Tax incentives:** The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) covers 30% of system cost for systems installed through 2032. Many states add additional incentives. Check the DSIRE database (dsireusa.org) for your state's programs.

After the federal credit, a $25,000 system costs $17,500. Many utility companies also offer rebates, further reducing costs.

**Payback period:** 7–12 years depending on your electricity rate, sun hours, and incentives. With current electricity price inflation, paybacks are shortening.

## Priority Equipment for Different Budgets

**Budget: Under $1,500**
Portable power station (1,000–2,000Wh) + 200W foldable solar panel = emergency essentials covered. Best ROI for most families.

**Budget: $3,000–5,000**
Larger portable power station (3,000+Wh) + 400W solar panels + EV charger adapter for car-to-home transfer if your car supports it. Covers 2–3 days of whole-house essential loads.

**Budget: $10,000–15,000**
Battery-only whole-home backup (Powerwall or equivalent). No solar panels needed; charges from grid. Provides seamless automatic backup with no action required during an outage.

**Budget: $20,000+**
Full solar + storage system. Best long-term ROI, maximum independence, federal tax credit applies.

## Tips for Maximizing Solar Output

**Panel placement:** South-facing panels at the optimal angle for your latitude get the most annual sun. For California (~35°N), that's about 35° tilt. Even east or west-facing panels produce 80–90% of south-facing output — don't let imperfect placement stop you.

**Shade is the enemy:** A shaded panel doesn't just lose its own output — in traditional string inverters, it can drag down the whole string. If shade is unavoidable, use microinverters (each panel works independently) or power optimizers.

**Keep panels clean:** Dust, pollen, and bird droppings reduce output. In dry climates especially, an annual cleaning keeps production near nameplate capacity.

**Battery temperature:** Most lithium batteries operate optimally between 32°F and 100°F. Extreme cold reduces capacity temporarily (you get it back as the battery warms up). Extreme heat over 100°F degrades batteries over time. For permanent installations in extreme climates, choose your battery location accordingly.
